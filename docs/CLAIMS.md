| Q1 | Is next-turn working state sparse and indexed lookup sublinear on real agent traces, *and* does the retrieved view preserve the turn? | Split, and the split is the finding. **Sparsity: yes.** On the 15,000-session public corpus the lexical/provenance index selects a median 12% of a 32K-128K history and its active fraction falls as history grows; on the longest sessions (median history 82,440 tokens, max 156,137) a 4,096-token active view is **5.0%** of history. **Fidelity: not at that size.** Teacher-forced next-turn scoring with a frozen Llama-3.2-1B shows a dose-response to the active budget - token-accuracy delta -10.1 pp (4,096 tokens), -6.0 pp (8,192), -0.26 pp (16,384) in the 8K-32K bucket - and at long histories 4,096 tokens costs -8.3 pp, i.e. the loss is **flat in history** but above the 2 pp tolerance until the view is ~16K tokens, which is 82% of an 8K-32K history. The gate therefore returns `kill` for small fixed budgets and `advance` only when the view approaches the history size.  The compiler is worth more than the budget at long histories: adding recency, oversized-span truncation and identifier provenance to the same index cuts the long-history loss at a fixed 4,096-token view from **-21.98 pp to -7.17 pp** (NLL +1.286 to +0.473) on the same 48 examples, and at 16,384 tokens it reaches **-1.08 pp at an active fraction of 0.199** (median history 82,440 tokens) - within tolerance, and with the fraction four times smaller than the 0.82 the same fidelity needed on 20K-token histories. End-task (not teacher-forced) quality is still required | `artifacts/g2-thoughtworks-structural-v1.json`, `artifacts/g2-model-quality-llama1b-long-v1.json`, `-b8192`, `-b16384`, `-longhist-b4096`, `artifacts/g2-compiler-lexical-b4096-n48.json`, `artifacts/g2-compiler-recency-b4096-n48.json`, `artifacts/g2-compiler-recency-b16384-n48.json` | G2 |

| Q3 | Can 1M/2K be cheaper to move than 32K/16K on hardware? | **Yes, measured.** 1M history with a 2K active set: ~0.10 s (0.053 s prefill + lookup) against 32K history with a 16K active set: ~0.49 s, a 5x separation set by the active set rather than the history. Against a full-KV move on this local 23.2-23.4 GB/s link, the 1M KV payload (12.00 GiB) costs 555.6 ms, so 1M/2K is 5.6x cheaper than moving it and 1M/16K is not; the crossover sits near 120 GB/s effective bandwidth | `artifacts/g3-hardware-primitives-qwen05b-v1.json`, `artifacts/g3-fullprefill-qwen05b-v1.json` | G3 |
| Q4 | Does replacing history-sized cold-route cost with measured active-set cost change the best routing decision under realistic skew/failure? | **Conditional, and measured: 4 of 48 replay cells advance.** With measured G3 costs and a declared workload, ephemeral mobility beats the strongest baseline (sticky-until-saturated with a KV-aware escape, or full-KV move) only when a worker is *removed* and the active set is 2,048 tokens (goodput x1.97, p50 2.46 s vs 2.48 s). At the 16,384-token active set G2 measured as fidelity-preserving, rematerialization (0.485 s) costs more than moving KV on this 23.2-23.4 GB/s fabric (0.017-0.556 s), so ephemeral is worse in every regime (goodput x0.16-0.75); a 10 or 2 GB/s fabric does not change that while sessions stay warm. | Q5 | Can a worker or a model be replaced without transferring history-sized state? | **Measured, first receipt.** With a median 130,131-token history, a model rollout resumes from the durable transcript + index for 0.25 ms of lookup plus **0.55 s** (Qwen2.5-0.5B) or **1.04 s** (Llama-3.2-1B) of active-set prefill on the *new* model, against **6.31 s** to re-prefill the history (optimistic linear scaling; infeasible at 512K+) - and the same durable view keeps the next turn's fidelity on a model that never saw the session (Llama-3.2-1B: token-accuracy delta +0.48 pp, NLL -0.010). End-task success and a real multi-worker failover are still open | `artifacts/g5-rollout-v1.json` | G5 |
